In Epicurus's letter to Pythocles, he proposes three different explanations for lightning strikes: compression of air by wind, a cloud suddenly breaking (he seems to imagine clouds as solid or at least sponge-like), or a buildup of fire (which he seems to imagine as a substance) suddenly bursting.

I suppose it's not any of these, it's... something to do with electricity?
Though I'm not sure why electricity does that.

But in any case, after giving his three explanations, Epicurus goes on to say (Makridis translation):

> It is indeed possible that the thunderbolt may be generated in any one of many more ways.

Maybe this is all you need if you're annoyed with people saying it's Zeus's wrath and they can tell you how to avoid it.
You just have to say, well, it could be a lot of other things too.

## Meeting unfounded explanations with more explanations

Maybe I used to think you shouldn't propose explanations without evidence.
Now I think you should have at least three.

Besides that it's more fun, that's what it means to lack evidence, right?
As evidence accumulates, all explanations but one are eventually falsified.

I do sometimes propose bullshit explanations adversarially, when I'm annoyed, as Epicurus was, at the unfounded claims of others.
When I say you lack evidence, the content of my claim Is that you cannot rule out other plausible alternatives.
The right way to make that case, at least in my opinion, is directly: list the alternatives.
Everything else (you haven't done a study, or you did a study but it wasn't randomized, etc. etc.) is at best a proxy for this substance.

But, again, it's fun to propose explanations.
When someone isn't taking what they're saying too seriously, I think I should meet them in the same spirit and play the same game.
